Cambodia's philosopher-king · Mahāyāna Buddhism

The king who felt his people's pain

Jayavarman VII freed Angkor after the Cham occupation of 1177 and was crowned in 1181. The first Khmer king devoted to Buddhism, he governed by the bodhisattva ideal: power exists to relieve suffering. He built Angkor Thom with the Bayon at its centre, Ta Prohm for his mother and Preah Khan for his father. His queens Jayarajadevi and, after her death, her sister Indradevi are thought to have deepened his Buddhist faith.

The bodily pain of his people was, for him, a sharper spiritual pain than his own, because the suffering of the public is the true suffering of a king.Paraphrase of his hospital inscriptions

Compassion as the purpose of power

Following Lokeśvara, the bodhisattva of compassion, the ruler's role is to vow to save beings from suffering. The calm smiling faces of the Bayon are widely read as this compassion watching over the kingdom in every direction.

ខ

The people's pain is the king's pain

A ruler should feel public suffering more keenly than private suffering. Legitimacy comes from care for the sick, the poor and the traveller, not from the king's own glory.

គ

Merit through public works

Hospitals with doctors and medicines, rest houses with fire along the roads, reservoirs and temples: giving to the public was both good government and the making of merit, offered for the welfare of all beings.

ឃ

Gratitude to parents and teachers

Ta Prohm honoured his mother as the Perfection of Wisdom and Preah Khan his father as Lokeśvara, joining family devotion, Buddhist virtue and the duty of kingship.
